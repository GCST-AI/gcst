01 · Framing

Every process model is answering the same three questions

You already have a project. Right now it lives in your head, a WhatsApp group, and one laptop that holds the only copy. That is not a process. That is a single point of failure with a group chat attached.

Question three is the one that separates the models. Everyone can plan. They differ only on what happens when the plan turns out to be wrong, which it will, around week three.

Hold on to that. Waterfall, iterative and RAD are not four philosophies competing for your loyalty. They are three different answers to one question, and the right answer depends on facts about your project that you can check today.

02 · Phase model

The Program Development Life Cycle

Six phases: requirements, design, implementation, testing, deployment, maintenance. Nobody disputes this list. Every model on this page runs all six. They disagree about the order you run them in and how many times.

The curve underneath is the important part. Those multipliers are the whole reason process models exist. A misunderstanding caught in a conversation costs one unit. The same misunderstanding caught by a user after release costs about a hundred, because by then it is buried under design decisions, code, tests and other people's work that all assumed it was right.

You will not feel this in a two-week assignment. You will feel it hard in a four-month project, which is what you are in now.

Stop and think · 01

Your project has no written requirements. Neither did the first version of WhatsApp. So what is the requirements phase actually buying, and who is it buying it for?

Two minutes. Write one sentence before you open this.

What I was listening for

Listen for the word later. A requirements document is insurance against a disagreement that has not happened yet.

If you are the only person who will ever touch the code and you ship on Friday, the premium is not worth paying. Change either of those two facts, add a second developer or add six months, and it becomes the cheapest thing you will buy all semester.

Now check your own situation. You are on a pod, so there is a second person. Your project runs past November, so there is time. Both facts are already true for you.

03 · Model 01

Waterfall

Every phase is completed and signed off before the next one starts. The output of one phase is the only input to the next. Water does not flow uphill, which is the whole point of the name.

Where it still wins

Launch vehicle software. Pacemaker firmware. Payment settlement. Anywhere the cost of being wrong in production is measured in lives or in crores, and the requirements genuinely do not move.

Where it fails you

Your project. You do not yet know what you are building, so a signed-off spec is just a guess with a signature on it.

The misreading

Waterfall is not slow at building. It is slow at changing its mind. Those are different weaknesses and only one of them is yours.

Stop and think · 02

A rocket launches once. A website deploys forty times a day. Waterfall is called obsolete and still ships rockets. What property of the problem decides the process?

Two minutes. One sentence. Name the property.

What I was listening for

The word you are looking for is reversibility.

If a bad decision can be undone cheaply, iterate and find out by doing. If it cannot be undone at all, spend the time up front, because up front is the only time you have.

Ask it of every decision in your project this week. Can I take this back on Monday? Most of yours you can, which tells you which model to use.

04 · Model 02

Iterative

Run the whole lifecycle in a short lap. Ship something real. Look at what happened. Run it again. The scope of a lap is small, but the list of phases inside it is the full six.

The rule

If a lap ends with nothing shipped, it was not a lap. It was two weeks.

Your lap 1

A page that loads and says what your project is. That is a real release. Ship it Friday.

The trade

You pay for it in rework, because you build on things you later change. You buy information with that money.

Stop and think · 03

Iteration means shipping something incomplete on purpose. Your instinct is to hide it until it is good. Which instinct protects the project, and which one protects you?

Two minutes. Be honest, nobody is grading this one.

What I was listening for

Hiding it protects you. That is not a character flaw, it is the normal reaction to being judged, and every professional developer has it.

But nothing you learn in private is feedback. A rough version in front of one real user on Friday tells you more than a perfect version in your head in November, and it tells you while you can still act on it.

05 · Model 03

Rapid Application Development

Fix the date, not the scope. Build rough prototypes of several modules in parallel, put each one in front of a real user, and let their reaction decide what the next prototype contains.

The trade

You spend code quality to buy information speed, and you do it deliberately. Prototypes are meant to be thrown away.

Needs

A real user who will look at it weekly, small teams that can work in parallel, and modules that can be built separately.

Breaks when

No user is available, or the system needs to be correct more than it needs to be liked. Nobody RADs a banking ledger.

Stop and think · 04

RAD trades code quality for speed and calls that a strategy. Name one thing in your project you should deliberately build badly this week. Now defend it.

Three minutes. I want a specific file or feature, not a category.

What I was listening for

The right answers are the parts you were going to throw away anyway. Hardcode the data. Fake the login. Skip the database and use a JavaScript array.

Build badly everything that only exists to prove the idea works, and build carefully the one thing your project is actually about.

If you cannot name which one that is, that is the real finding of this exercise, and it is worth more than the answer would have been.

06 · Side by side

Same team, same project, one requirement changes in week three

Notice that waterfall is not slower at building. It is slower at changing its mind. If your requirements really were frozen, the top lane would deliver first and cost least. They are not frozen, which is why the top lane loses.

07 · Part two · Definition

Autocomplete predicts. An agent runs a loop.

Everything above was invented before any of you were born and none of it has been repealed. What changed is how fast one person can move through a lap.

This matters for your project because the loop is the same loop you saw in the iterative model, only running in minutes instead of weeks. The skill you are actually developing is not prompting. It is writing a stopping condition clear enough that a loop can tell when it is done, which is the same skill as writing a requirement.

Test yourself on that at home. Take the next thing you want to build and write down, in one sentence, how anyone would know it is finished. If you cannot, the agent cannot either, and you will get back something that looks right and is not.

08 · Honest accounting

It removes typing, not judgment

Map the tool onto the lifecycle and the picture is lopsided. Agents are strongest exactly where you were already competent and weakest where you were already struggling.

The requirements phase just became the phase that matters most. That is the opposite of what most of you expected. When implementation is cheap, the cost of building the wrong thing goes up, not down, because you will build it faster and further before anyone notices.

The 100× multiplier from section 02 does not care that an agent wrote the code.

09 · The tool

Four places your code lives

A commit is a labelled snapshot of the whole project that you can return to. That is the actual promise here. It means you can break things confidently, which is the only way anyone learns to build anything.

git clone <url>   # copy the shared repo onto your laptop
git add index.html   # choose what goes in the next snapshot
git commit -m "add project section"   # take the snapshot
git push   # send your snapshots to GitHub
git pull   # get everyone else's
You do not need any of this to do the homework

Learn the five commands, they are the professional path and you will need them. But the class site can be edited entirely in your browser, with no install, and that is what the next section walks you through. Do the browser version tonight. Learn the commands this month.

10 · Do this now · 30 minutes

Ship v0.1 into the class site

Your project has a permanent address. It is https://gcst-ai.github.io/gcst/projects/pod-X/, where X is your pod number. That address never changes, and tonight you are going to make it say something true.

The steps

  1. Open github.com/GCST-AI/gcst and sign in. Read the existing index.html before you change anything.
  2. Go into projects/ and open your pod folder, for example pod-07.
  3. Click index.html, then the pencil icon at the top right.
  4. Change the words between the tags. <h1>Pod 7</h1> becomes <h1>Crop Disease Scanner</h1>. Change the words, keep the tags.
  5. Click the green Commit changes button, then Commit changes again.
  6. Wait two minutes, open your pod address, and refresh. That is a deployment.
  7. Now do the same to the front page: find your pod's card in the top-level index.html and put your real project name and one sentence in it.
What v0.1 means

Not finished. Loading. A page that opens, says what your project is called, and says in one sentence what it does. If you are tempted to wait until it looks good, reread section 04 and the honest answer you wrote there.

12 · Close

Which one does your project actually need?

  1. The lifecycle is the list. The models are arguments about the order and the loop.
  2. Reversibility picks the model. Cheap to undo means iterate. Impossible to undo means plan first.
  3. Ship the lap, not the feature. A lap that ships nothing was not a lap.
  4. The agent removes typing, not judgment. Requirements is now the phase that matters most.
